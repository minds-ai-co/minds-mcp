Synthetic research.
Ask for the evidence.

Choose evidence that fits your decision before choosing a synthetic market research vendor.

Prepared 25 October 2026 · Published by Minds

This is a procurement planning resource, not a ranked “best tools” test or a 2027 product review. Minds is a vendor included in the worksheet. No vendor has a prefilled score.

Start with the editable worksheet

Four tabs cover instructions, vendor evidence, evaluation criteria and matched numeric results. Open the Sheet and choose File → Make a copy. Keep customer data and private vendor responses in your own restricted copy.

Open Google Sheets template Read without signing in

Prefer portable files? Download the vendor evidence CSV, evaluation checklist CSV or matched results CSV. The CSVs contain no formulas; the native Sheet calculates numeric gaps.

Blank evidence means not assessed. It is not a vendor failure or a recommendation.

Define the decision first

Write down the intended audience, question, outcome metric and acceptable error before reviewing vendor results. Your test should establish whether the approach helps with that decision, beyond an example that sounds plausible.

If you have 23 synthetic answers and no human results

The comparison is still unmeasured. Obtain a matching human reference:

  1. Run the same questions with a suitable human sample, using the same screening, stimuli, options and presentation.
  2. Alternatively, use an existing human dataset when its population and questions match sufficiently. Document material mismatches and the collection date.
  3. Reserve questions or outcomes as a holdout. Keep reference answers out of generation and tuning.

Choose sample sizes and uncertainty analysis for your decision. Record subgroup sizes, sampling limitations and repeated-run variation; this template makes no universal sample-size promise.

Compare the same question, audience and unit

For each outcome, record the source, date, model/run details, human result, synthetic result and uncertainty. A human share of 40% and a synthetic share of 46% differ by 6 percentage points. That is not 6% relative error or an overall accuracy score.

Record what the evidence supports

Document what was tested, what was missing, where the approach failed, the acceptable use and the reviewer. A question-level gap is not a general accuracy claim.
